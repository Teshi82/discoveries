Northern Honshu

Tohoku

Mountain streams, snow and hot-spring steam: Japan's quiet heartland.

The six prefectures of Tohoku, in the north of Honshu, are a land of deep forests and mountains, rustic hot springs and powerful summer festivals. There are fewer travellers than in the big cities, so you can take your time with the landscapes and local life.

Highlights

  1. Oirase Gorge and Lake Towada

    A mountain stream that flows about 14 km from Lake Towada. A walking trail follows the water past mossy rocks and waterfalls.

  2. Zao

    In winter, frost-covered trees known as "snow monsters" blanket the slopes, at their best in January and February. In summer, head for Okama, the emerald crater lake.

  3. Matsushima

    One of Japan's Three Scenic Views, a bay scattered with more than 260 islands. Tour it by sightseeing boat and visit Zuiganji, the temple associated with Date Masamune.

  4. Ginzan Onsen

    A hot-spring town where wooden inns from the Taisho and early Showa eras line both banks of a river. It is especially atmospheric at dusk, when the gas lamps are lit, and in the snow.

Trip notes

Best time
Fresh greenery in May and June, summer festivals in early August, autumn leaves in October, snow monsters in January and February. The trick is to pick your destination by season.
Getting there
By Shinkansen from Tokyo: about 1 hour 30 minutes to Sendai and about 3 hours 10 minutes to Shin-Aomori.
How long to stay
3–5 days. Travel between prefectures takes time, so two or three prefectures per trip is a comfortable pace.

Travel tip

Buses beyond the stations are infrequent, so check timetables in advance or rent a car. Book accommodation early for the festival period in early August.
