Northern Japan

Hokkaido

Flower-covered hills in summer, powder snow in winter: a northern land that changes with every season.

Covering about a fifth of Japan's land area, Hokkaido is all wide skies, straight roads and untouched nature. Summers are cool and winters bring deep snow. The sights are far apart, so plan around one or two areas.

Highlights

  1. Furano and Biei

    Lavender fields that peak in mid-July, and the "patchwork hills" where crops paint patterns across the land. Biei's Blue Pond is another favourite.

  2. Shiretoko

    A peninsula inscribed as a World Natural Heritage site in 2005. In summer, walk the Shiretoko Five Lakes or take a sightseeing boat; in winter, see drift ice on the Sea of Okhotsk.

  3. Niseko

    A powder-snow mecca that draws skiers from around the world. In summer, go rafting or hiking with views of Mt. Yotei.

  4. Sapporo and Otaru

    The Sapporo Snow Festival in early February, and Otaru's canal lined with stone warehouses. Miso ramen and fresh seafood are part of the pleasure.

Trip notes

Best time
June to August for flowers and rolling hills; December to March for snow and winter sports. Autumn colours begin in the Daisetsuzan mountains in mid-September.
Getting there
About 1 hour 30 minutes from Tokyo (Haneda) to New Chitose Airport. From the airport, a rapid train reaches Sapporo in about 40 minutes.
How long to stay
4–7 days. For a single trip, focus on one or two areas, such as central or eastern Hokkaido.

Travel tip

Distances are greater than they look on the map: about 2 hours by car from Sapporo to Furano, and about 6 hours to Shiretoko. If you are not used to driving on snow, take trains and buses in winter.
