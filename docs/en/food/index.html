More than scenery

Food

Sushi, ramen, regional cooking. Taste the seasons and the local produce, from street stalls to century-old restaurants.

Japanese food changes greatly with place and season. In 2013, washoku, traditional Japanese cuisine, was added to UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list. There are discoveries not only in fine restaurants but in station lunch boxes, department store food halls and convenience stores.

Key points

  1. Sushi

    From affordable conveyor-belt sushi to counters where the chef shapes each piece in front of you. For counter restaurants, it is usual to reserve and order omakase, the chef's selection.

  2. Ramen

    Miso in Sapporo, pork-bone broth in Hakata, soy sauce in Tokyo: the flavour differs by region. Many shops have you buy a meal ticket from a machine at the entrance.

  3. Izakaya

    Casual pubs for sharing small plates over drinks. A small appetiser called otoshi may be served in place of a table charge.

  4. Regional dishes and markets

    Takoyaki in Osaka, oysters in Hiroshima, seafood rice bowls in Hokkaido. Local markets are where you can taste a region from early morning.

Trip notes

Travel tip

No tipping. Eat street food in front of the shop or in a designated area, not while walking. If you have allergies or are vegetarian, tell the restaurant in advance: even vegetable dishes may use fish stock.
