Kyoto

Walk the thousand-year capital in the quiet of early morning.

Japan's capital for more than a thousand years from 794, Kyoto is still full of World Heritage temples and shrines, gardens, and lanes lined with wooden townhouses. The sights are spread across the city, so giving each district its own day makes for an easy pace.

Highlights

  1. Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)

    A pavilion covered in gold leaf, mirrored in its pond: the classic Kyoto view. The reflection is at its sharpest on a clear morning.

  2. Fushimi Inari Taisha

    Vermilion torii gates run in tunnels up the mountainside. The full loop of Mt. Inari takes about two hours, and the crowds thin the higher you climb.

  3. Arashiyama

    A path through towering bamboo and the Togetsukyo Bridge over the Katsura River. The whole hillside turns colour in cherry blossom and autumn leaf seasons.

  4. Kiyomizu-dera and Higashiyama

    Look out over the city from the temple's famous wooden stage on the hillside, then wander the stone-paved lanes of Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka.

Trip notes

Best time
Cherry blossoms from late March to early April; autumn leaves from mid-November to early December. Fresh green May and snowy winter days are comparatively calm.
Getting there
About 2 hours 15 minutes from Tokyo by Shinkansen. From Kansai International Airport, the Haruka limited express takes about 1 hour 20 minutes.
How long to stay
2–3 days. Add another day for Nara or Uji.

Travel tip

Visit the popular temples and shrines right at opening time. City buses get crowded, so combining the subway, trains and walking is often smoother.
