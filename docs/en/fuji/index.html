Yamanashi & Shizuoka

Mt. Fuji & the Fuji Five Lakes

Mirrored in lakes, wrapped in cloud: a trip to gaze at Japan's highest mountain.

Mt. Fuji, 3,776 m high, was inscribed as a World Cultural Heritage site in 2013. The Fuji Five Lakes along its northern foot are the best places to see it across the water. Even without climbing, simply changing your viewpoint can fill a whole day.

Highlights

  1. Lake Kawaguchiko

    The gateway to the Fuji Five Lakes. On windless mornings an upside-down Fuji appears on the surface. Oishi Park on the north shore frames the mountain with flowers.

  2. Arakurayama Sengen Park

    Climb 398 steps to the observation deck where a five-storied pagoda and Mt. Fuji fit in a single frame. It is especially popular in cherry blossom season.

  3. Oshino Hakkai

    Eight ponds fed by Fuji's snowmelt, which takes many years to filter through the ground. The water is clear enough to see right to the bottom.

  4. Fuji 5th Station and climbing

    The trails are usually open from early July to early September. Fees and access limits apply, so check the official site before you plan.

Trip notes

Best time
The mountain is most often visible in the crisp air of November to February. See it with cherry blossoms in April, or with autumn leaves in early to mid-November.
Getting there
From Shinjuku to Kawaguchiko: about 1 hour 45 minutes by highway bus, or about 2 hours on the Fuji Excursion limited express.
How long to stay
1–2 days. Stay a night by the lake to catch Fuji in the morning.

Travel tip

Clouds tend to build in the afternoon, so early morning is your best chance. Checking a live camera before you set out is reassuring.
