Chugoku & Shikoku

Setouchi

Travel a calm sea and its islands, linked by bridges and boats.

The Seto Inland Sea, enclosed by Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu, is dotted with islands large and small. The climate is mild with little rain, and old harbours and streetscapes survive in the seaside towns. Bicycles, boats and contemporary art are the stars of a trip here.

Highlights

  1. Shimanami Kaido

    A cycling route of about 70 km linking Onomichi in Hiroshima Prefecture with Imabari in Ehime Prefecture across six islands and their bridges. Rental bikes can be returned on islands along the way.

  2. Naoshima

    An art island known for the Chichu Art Museum, designed by Tadao Ando, and Yayoi Kusama's Pumpkin on the shore. It is about 20 minutes by ferry from Uno Port in Okayama Prefecture.

  3. Miyajima and Itsukushima Shrine

    A vermilion torii gate and shrine that seem to float on the sea at high tide. At low tide you can walk right up to the gate. It is a World Heritage site.

  4. Kurashiki Bikan Historical Quarter

    White-walled storehouses line a willow-fringed canal. The Ohara Museum of Art, Japan's first private museum of Western art, is here too.

Trip notes

Best time
Spring (March to May) and autumn (October to November) are the most comfortable. In years when the Setouchi Triennale is held, once every three years, the islands are livelier still.
Getting there
By Shinkansen from Tokyo: about 3 hours 20 minutes to Okayama, about 4 hours to Hiroshima. Ferries and high-speed boats run to the islands from ports around the region.
How long to stay
3–5 days. Allow at least 4 days to cover both Naoshima and the Shimanami Kaido.

Travel tip

Many museums close on Mondays, and the Chichu Art Museum requires advance reservations. Ferries are limited, so check your return time first.
